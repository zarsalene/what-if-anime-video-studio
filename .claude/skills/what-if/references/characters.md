# Original anime characters

Design a small original cast only when a person helps explain the science. A narrator can remain off-screen. Characters should support the scenario rather than become a generic mascot.

## Model sheet

For every recurring character, record:

- age range, build, height and silhouette;
- skin tone, face shape, eye shape, eyebrow shape and hairstyle;
- a compact clothing palette that reads against every background;
- front, profile and three-quarter views;
- neutral, curious, worried, startled and relieved expressions;
- the scale of the character beside a familiar object.

Keep the same design across every shot. Preserve eye spacing, hair silhouette, clothing details and proportions. If a generated image tool is used, repeat the model-sheet description and visual reference; repair drift rather than accepting inconsistent faces.

## Visual language

- Use expressive eyes with iris, pupil, catchlight and a readable upper lid. Shape the brows and mouth to carry emotion.
- Use clear inked contours with a deliberate hierarchy: strongest around the focal character, lighter on distant objects.
- Shade with a small number of cel-shaded light and shadow shapes. Put highlights on hair, eyes, metal and glass with restraint.
- Make the pose readable from its silhouette. Exaggerate a reaction for one beat, then settle it.
- Distinguish characters through silhouette, hair, face and clothing design instead of random accessories or color alone.
- Avoid featureless black bodies, white oval eyes without pupils, stick limbs, blank faces and toy-proportioned bodies unless the user explicitly asks for that look.

## Acting and motion

Plan eye direction, blink timing, head angle and hand pose for each line of narration. Use anticipation before a sudden event, a brief impact pose, then a settling reaction. Keep secondary motion small and tied to the action: hair, sleeves, loose objects and clothing follow through after the body.

Use smooth eased motion for camera or ordinary movement. Reserve held frames, speed lines and impact frames for one or two dramatic moments. Avoid constant bouncing or motion that competes with the explanation.

## Staging

Keep faces and essential diagrams out of captions and interface overlays. Establish the character's scale beside the event. For a planet, weather system or city-wide effect, begin with a human or landmark for scale, then widen to show the consequence.

For crowds, vary body shape, clothing and pose. Do not duplicate one character asset across a crowd without meaningful changes.
