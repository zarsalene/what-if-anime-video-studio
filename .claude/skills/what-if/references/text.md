# Narration captions and science graphics

Narration is the main explanation. Captions make it readable without sound and must stay synchronized with the TTS.

## Captions

- Caption every spoken line. Break at natural phrases, not one word per frame.
- Keep each card to one or two short lines with strong contrast and a readable mobile size.
- Highlight only the key noun, verb or number. Keep the highlight color consistent with the scene's palette.
- Place captions away from faces, diagrams, the YouTube interface safe areas and visual action.
- Show units with numbers; define uncommon terms with a short label.
- Do not make a caption carry facts the narration never says unless it is a source or diagram label.

## Graphic language

Use a small consistent set of tools:

- **Mechanism label:** a short phrase attached to the object it names.
- **Scale marker:** a familiar size comparison with a clear source if numerical.
- **Map/diagram:** simple and accurate; highlight one region or direction at a time.
- **Timeline:** show only when timing is central to the scenario.
- **Impact card:** one concise number or consequence at the reveal.
- **Question/end card:** return to the original What If question and give the answer or a thoughtful final question.

Use clean anime title lettering with a deliberate font pair and limited outlines/shadows. Avoid multiple simultaneous text styles, decorative Japanese characters without meaning, faux subtitles, dense paragraphs and flashing text. All words must be spelled, sourced when factual, and legible on a phone.
