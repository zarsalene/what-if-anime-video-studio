# Vertical What If Shorts

Default to 9:16 (1080×1920) and 30 fps unless the user specifies another source frame rate. A clear target is 30–60 seconds. YouTube currently classifies square or vertical videos up to 3 minutes as Shorts; confirm the current official limit before export using the link in `originality-and-youtube.md`.

## Frame for a phone

- Keep the focal action near the center safe area. Avoid tiny diagrams, thin lines and details that disappear at phone size.
- Leave room for YouTube interface controls at the right and the lower caption area.
- Use a large, clear first image that communicates the question before the viewer reads the title.
- Keep captions short and high contrast. Test them on a small preview.
- Make each shot understandable quickly; use a scale reference and one action at a time.

## Short structure

1. Ask the What If question immediately.
2. Show the changed condition.
3. Explain the first consequence.
4. Escalate the chain with two or three visual steps.
5. Land on the largest supported consequence.
6. Give a short answer or final question.

Do not speed the narration until it becomes hard to understand. Remove secondary facts instead. Keep a pause before the strongest reveal.

## Export and cover

Export a single vertical master with narration, synchronized captions and a cover frame that matches the video. Verify current Shorts duration and encoding guidance in YouTube's official help before rendering. Review the first and last frame, full-length pacing, caption safe areas and loudness.
