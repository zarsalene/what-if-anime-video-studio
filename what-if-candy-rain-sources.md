# Sources — What If the Sky Rained Candy?

- NOAA, [Clouds](https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream/clouds): cloud particles are minute water droplets and/or ice crystals.
- NOAA, [Precipitation](https://prod-01-alb-www-noaa.woc.noaa.gov/jetstream/atmosphere/precipitation): cloud water droplets and ice crystals grow before becoming heavy enough to fall as precipitation.
- U.S. Geological Survey, [Dissolved Oxygen and Water](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/dissolved-oxygen-and-water): aquatic organisms need dissolved oxygen, and bacteria can consume oxygen as organic matter decays.

The candy cloud and candy precipitation are explicitly fictional. Claims about physical impacts are conditional on unknown piece size, shape, mass and fall speed. The waterway effect is presented as a possible consequence of a sufficiently large sugar load; no specific environmental outcome is claimed without a defined amount and dilution.
